THE COMPREHENSIVE COMPASS OF THE BHAGAVAD GITA
SECTION 1: THE FOUNDATION OF ABSOLUTE REALITY

(Based on Chapter 13: Kshetra & Kshetrajna)

Before understanding action or psychology, one must analyze the components of existence. Reality is divided into the vehicle (the observed), the conscious passenger (the observer), and the ultimate guide.

    The Kshetra (The Field): The physical body, the material mind, and the external temporary elements of nature. It acts as the passive vehicle through which worldly experiences happen.

    The Individual Kshetrajna (The Knower of the Field / Atma): The individual spirit soul residing inside the body. It is the real conscious observer, but its awareness is restricted only to its own specific, individual body and experiences.

    The Supreme Kshetrajna (The Paramatma): Lord Krishna Himself, who simultaneously resides inside the hearts of all living entities. He is the ultimate Knower, Witness, and well-wishing guide, completely aware of all bodies, fields, and thoughts in existence.

SECTION 2: THE ENVIRONMENT — THE THREE MODES OF NATURE (GUNAS)

(Based on Chapters 14 & 17)

The moment the individual soul (Atma) is placed inside the material vehicle (Kshetra), it is immediately conditioned and influenced by three universal forces called Gunas. These forces dictate human lifestyle, psychological health, choices, and material attachments.
A. Sattva Guna (The Mode of Goodness) — The Pious Environment

    Psychology & Feelings: Increases happiness, purity, cleanliness, forgiveness, tolerance, and mental clarity. Actions are done cleanly and truthfully out of a sense of duty.

    Food (Ahara): Fresh, wholesome, and strictly vegetarian. Foods that are sweet, juicy, fatty, and naturally palatable (like grains, milk, fruits), which increase longevity and health.

    Austerity (Tapas): Self-purification performed with unwavering faith, focusing on control of body and speech without expecting material rewards.

    Charity (Dana): Given strictly out of a sense of duty to a worthy person, at the right time and place, expecting absolutely nothing in return.

B. Rajas Guna (The Mode of Passion) — The Driven/Stressed Environment

    Psychology & Feelings: Shifts consciousness toward intense endeavor, uncontrollable longings, material greed, stress, and heavy attachment to results. Modern society often misinterprets this frantic, passionate mode as "advancement," which leads to anxiety.

    Food (Ahara): Excessively bitter, sour, salty, highly pungent, dry, or burning hot. These directly cause distress, pain, and disease.

    Austerity (Tapas): Undertaken ostentatiously out of pride, vanity, or showmanship simply to gain honor, prestige, and public worship.

    Charity (Dana): Given grudgingly, reluctantly, or with the secret expectation of a return favor, tax write-off, or public fame.

C. Tamas Guna (The Mode of Ignorance/Darkness) — The Degraded Environment

    Psychology & Feelings: Triggers deep laziness, madness, inertia, illusion, chronic procrastination, and a dependency on intoxication or hallucination. Actions are chaotic and disrespectful.

    Food (Ahara): Cooked more than 3 hours prior, stale, cold, putrid, decomposed, or consisting of unclean remnants (non-vegetarian/animal slaughter fruits).

    Austerity (Tapas): Performed out of foolish delusion, involving whimsical self-torture or acts designed to harm and destroy others.

    Charity (Dana): Given at an improper place or time to unworthy, degrading causes, or given with open disrespect and contempt to the recipient.

SECTION 3: THE INTERNAL REACTION — THE GENERATION OF THE ANARTHAS

(Based on Chapter 3 & psychological degradation)

Kama – Lust / Self-centered desire / Craving for independent material enjoyment

Krodha – Anger / Wrath (which arises when your material desires are blocked)

Lobha – Greed / Misplaced claim of ownership (the desire to accumulate more than needed)

Moha – Illusion / Delusion / Deep confusion about your true spiritual identity

Mada – Pride / False Ego (blindly assuming that you are the sole doer of everything)

Matsarya – Envy / Malice / Competing against the universal and divine order


Total Gunas (Forces of Nature) = 3 (satva Goodness, rajasa Passion, tamasa Ignorance)

Total Anarthas (Enemies of the Mind) = 6 (The list above)

SECTION 4: THE DIRECTION — THE THREE-FOLD CLASSIFICATION OF ACTIONS (KARMA)

(Based on Chapter 4)

Driven by either the Gunas or the Anarthas, the human driver steers the vehicle through actions. Every activity falls into one of three distinct categories, determining future karmic bondage or freedom:

    Karma (Fruitive / Materially Bound Actions): Activities performed according to scriptural laws, but driven by a desire to enjoy the rewards (e.g., working for wealth, a good family, or heavenly elevation). Because any action driven by material desire produces a reaction, you must take another material body to enjoy those fruits, trapping you in the loop of birth and death.

    Vikarma (Forbidden / Whimsical Actions): Whimsical actions that break scriptural rules and guidelines (e.g., acts of violence, scams, or meat-eating). These generate heavy sinful reactions, dragging the soul down into lower species or lower regions of the cosmos to face natural justice.

    Akarma (Transcendental Inaction): The highest level of action, also called Buddhi Yoga. It means performing your prescribed duties faithfully, but completely abandoning attachment to the success, failure, or fruits of the work, dedicating everything to the Supreme. Because you claim no personal ownership over the results, it creates zero material reactions—effectively burning up past karmic bondage and leading to liberation.

SECTION 5: THE ESCAPE ROUTE — THE YOGA LADDER OF PROGRESS

(The Spiritual Solution across the text)

To cure the internal enemies (Anarthas), overcome the lower environment (Gunas), and convert ordinary actions into liberating Akarma, Lord Krishna offers a custom-tailored spiritual ladder. A seeker climbs this ladder depending on their internal maturity:

    Karma Yoga (The Path of Selfless Action): Designed for those with material desires. Instead of prematurely abandoning your work or family, you continue acting according to your duty (Swadharma), but you sacrifice and renounce the fruits of your labor for the Supreme. This selflessness purifies the heart.

    Sankhya Yoga / Gyana Yoga (The Path of Knowledge): Designed for the intellectually inclined. Through strict empirical and analytical study of the material world, you learn to philosophically separate the eternal soul from the temporary body, anchoring yourself in the realization: "I am not this material body; I am an eternal spirit soul (Atma-gayan)."

    Ashtanga Yoga / Dhyana Yoga (The Path of Meditation): For those seeking severe internal mastery. Practitioners use breath control (Pranayama) and strict mental withdrawal to silence the physical senses, focusing their internal gaze entirely on reaching Samadhi—intense absorption on the form of the Supreme Knower (Paramatma) residing in the heart.

    Bhakti Yoga / Buddhi Yoga (The Path of Devotional Service): Highlighted by Krishna as the topmost, easiest, and most comprehensive rung of the spiritual ladder. Instead of trying to forcefully crush or repress your thoughts, senses, and desires, you simply channel them directly into the loving service of the Supreme Lord. By experiencing this spiritual "higher taste," all lower material desires and the six Anarthas naturally wither away, bringing permanent liberation and returning the soul to its eternal home.
